Why Flatten a PDF?

Lock a filled form before submitting

Once you have filled in your tax form, government application or contract, flattening ensures the recipient cannot alter the values. The form data becomes part of the page — uneditable and permanent.

Ensure consistent display across viewers

Interactive form fields sometimes render differently across PDF readers. A flattened PDF looks identical in every viewer — macOS Preview, Chrome's built-in PDF viewer, mobile apps — because it contains only static content.

Prepare PDFs for printing or archiving

Some print services and archiving systems expect flat PDFs without interactive layers. Flattening removes all form metadata, widgets and JavaScript — leaving a clean, universally compatible document.

Remove hidden field data

PDF form fields store their values as structured metadata. If you share a filled form without flattening, a recipient could inspect the raw PDF and see field names, original values, or metadata attached to the form object. Flattening removes all of that.

When to Flatten: Common Document Types

Flattening is the right step whenever you share a filled document that must not be changed by the recipient. The most frequent use cases:

  • Tax forms (W-9, W-2, 1040, self-assessment) — flatten before emailing to your accountant or employer so the values cannot be altered.
  • Government and visa applications — many agencies require a flattened PDF for digital submission or printing from a kiosk.
  • Rental agreements and contracts — lock the signed version so neither party can later claim different terms were entered.
  • Insurance claim forms — bake all field values into the page before uploading to your insurer's portal.
  • Medical intake and consent forms — personal health data embedded in static content cannot be extracted by inspecting raw form fields.
  • Annotated PDFs with highlights, stamps, or comments — flatten annotations so they appear in every viewer, including those that do not support PDF markup layers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does flattening a PDF actually do?

Flattening merges all interactive layers — form fields, annotations, digital signatures, and overlays — into the static page content. The result is a flat, non-interactive PDF that looks identical to the original but cannot be edited.

Does PDFree store or log my files after processing?

No. PDFree runs entirely in your browser — there is no backend server that receives your files. Because nothing is uploaded, nothing can be stored, logged, or retained. Your document exists only in your browser's memory for the duration of the session and is cleared when you close the tab.

Can I reverse a flattened PDF?

No. Flattening is irreversible — the original form fields are permanently replaced by static visual content. Always keep a backup of your original filled PDF before flattening if you think you might need to change values later.

Does flattening remove digital signatures?

Drawn signatures created with the Fill PDF tool (signature pad images) are flattened into the page. Cryptographic digital signatures from certified e-signature providers may be handled differently — the visual appearance is preserved but the cryptographic certificate may no longer validate after flattening.

Is my PDF uploaded to any server?

No. Everything runs in your browser using pdf-lib. Neither your original PDF nor the flattened result is ever sent anywhere. Your document and its contents stay on your device at all times.

Why flatten instead of just saving the filled PDF?

A saved filled PDF still contains editable fields — a recipient could clear your values and re-fill them. Flattening permanently locks the content into the page, prevents editing, ensures consistent rendering across all PDF viewers, and removes hidden form metadata from the file.

How do I flatten a PDF in my browser?

PDFree Flatten runs in any modern browser — Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge — on Mac, Windows, and Linux. No download or installation required. Open the Flatten PDF tool, load your PDF, click Flatten, and the result downloads directly to your device. No desktop software needed at any step.

Does flattening a PDF reduce file size?

Often slightly. Flattening removes interactive form widget data and JavaScript, which reduces file size for forms with many fields. However, images and embedded fonts are unaffected. For significant size reduction, run the flattened output through the Compress PDF tool.

What is the difference between flattening and password-protecting a PDF?

They solve different problems. Flattening removes the interactive form structure entirely — no fields exist to edit, regardless of permissions. Password protection restricts access or editing rights but leaves form fields intact; someone with the password could still modify a fillable form. For maximum security, flatten first to bake the content, then add a password with Protect PDF.
