When You Need to Resize a PDF

A US Letter PDF needs to print on A4 (or vice versa)

A document made for one region's default paper size prints slightly off or gets cropped on the other. Resize it to match the paper actually loaded in your printer.

Contracts and reports need a consistent, professional look

Business documents assembled from multiple sources sometimes mix page sizes. Resize every page to the same paper size before sending or printing a client-facing PDF.

School or academic formatting requirements

Assignments and submissions often require a specific paper size. Fit your document to A4 or Letter before printing or uploading it.

Forms, patterns, or diagrams that must stay true to scale

Some documents — sewing patterns, official forms, scaled diagrams — break if they're resized at all. Use Actual size mode to place the original content on a new paper size without scaling a single pixel.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does resizing a PDF upload my file to a server?

No. Every page is rebuilt entirely in your browser. Your file is never sent anywhere.

Why does my PDF print cut off even though it looks fine on screen?

Usually the page size in the file doesn't match the paper loaded in the printer, and the browser or driver's automatic "Fit to Page" scaling is guessing how to reconcile the two — inconsistently. Resizing the actual PDF to your target paper size removes that guesswork permanently.

What's the difference between Fit, Fill, and Actual size?

Fit shrinks the page so all of it is visible on the new paper size, leaving a border if the proportions don't match exactly. Fill scales up to cover the entire new page edge-to-edge, which can crop a small amount off the sides. Actual size applies no scaling at all — the original content is centered on the new paper size at true scale, which is correct for patterns, forms, or diagrams that must not change size.

What does "Auto" orientation do?

Auto matches the new page's orientation to each source page's own orientation — a landscape page gets a landscape target, a portrait page gets a portrait target — so a wide spreadsheet printout isn't squeezed into a tall frame at a tiny scale. You can also force Portrait or Landscape for every page.

Is there a limit on how many pages I can resize?

No. There's no page-count or file-size limit, and no per-hour task limit. Resize a 2-page form or a 500-page report as many times as you need.

Does resizing reduce the quality of images or text?

No. Each page is embedded as a vector object and scaled mathematically, not re-rendered as a raster image — text stays sharp and searchable, and images keep their original resolution relative to the new scale.

Can I resize a PDF that mixes Letter and A4 pages?

Yes. Every page is measured and fit individually, so a document with mixed original page sizes still converges to one uniform paper size in the output.

Can I resize multiple PDF files at once?
