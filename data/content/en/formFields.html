How to Add Form Fields to a PDF in 3 Steps

1
Open your PDF

Click Choose files or drag your PDF into the drop zone. PDFree checks whether the document already has interactive fields — if it does, you're pointed to the Fill tool instead, since this tool is only for documents that don't have any fields yet.

2
Click to place fields

Click anywhere on the page to drop a text field. Drag it to reposition, drag the corner handle to resize, and type a name directly into the field. Repeat for every blank on the page — and every page, using the page navigation above the preview. Zoom in for precise placement over small printed blanks, and use the Placed fields list beside the page to jump straight to any field you've already added, on any page.

3
Download the fillable PDF

Click Add Fields & Download. PDFree writes real AcroForm text fields into the document in your browser and downloads the result directly to your device — no waiting, no email, no account.

Why Add Form Fields Here Instead of a Cloud Tool?

Most online tools that claim to make a PDF fillable require uploading your document to a remote server, where a copy sits until someone deletes it. PDFree works differently: the entire operation — rendering the page you click on, and writing the AcroForm fields into the output — runs inside your browser using pdf.js and pdf-lib, the same open-source libraries powering PDFree's other tools. Your PDF never leaves your device.

The fields this tool creates aren't a visual trick, either. Some tools "make a PDF fillable" by drawing a flat text box wherever you click and baking it into the page as a picture — it looks like a field but isn't one. PDFree writes an actual AcroForm text field into the PDF's structure, the same kind Acrobat creates, so the result opens correctly and stays genuinely interactive in any standards-compliant PDF reader — including PDFree's own Fill tool.
