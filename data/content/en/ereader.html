Why This Isn't "Reflow" — And Why That's the Honest Answer

Some PDF readers advertise a "reflow" mode that re-wraps text to fit a smaller screen, the way an EPUB does. In practice it rarely works well, because a PDF's page layout isn't stored as flowing text the way EPUB's HTML-based format is — it's a fixed arrangement of glyphs and coordinates, and reconstructing paragraph structure from that is unreliable at best. Rather than promise a reflow that disappoints, the E-Reader Optimizer does something more dependable: it finds the actual content on each page — using the median across sampled pages so one outlier photo or blank divider page can't throw off the whole book — trims the margins down to that content, and fits the result to your device's screen ratio. The output is an image-based PDF, the same trade-off PDFree's Clean Scan tool makes for restoring scans: text is no longer selectable or searchable, but every page reads clean and full-screen on your device.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does optimizing a PDF upload my file to a server?

No. Every page is rendered, cropped, and rebuilt entirely in your browser. Your file is never sent anywhere.

Will the text still be selectable or searchable afterward?

No — the output is a rasterized, image-based PDF, the same trade-off PDFree's Clean Scan tool makes. Cropping and reflowing a real fixed-layout PDF without turning it into images isn't reliably possible; if you need to keep selectable text, don't run it through this tool.

How does the automatic margin detection work?

PDFree samples pages across the document, detects each one's actual content area, and takes the median across those samples — so a single full-bleed photo or a near-blank divider page can't drag the crop box too tight or too loose for the rest of the book.

Which devices are the presets tuned for?

Kindle (6"/7" screens), reMarkable and Onyx Boox (10.3" screens), and Kobo/Nook readers. Each preset matches the target device's screen aspect ratio so pages fill the screen without letterboxing more than necessary.

Could this crop off real content, like a footnote near the edge?

The crop only ever grows outward from the detected content box toward your device's aspect ratio — it never shrinks further to force a fit. If a page's content sits right at the edge of the detected box, check the live preview before downloading.

Is there a limit on how many pages I can optimize?

No. There's no page-count or file-size limit, and no per-hour task limit. Optimize a single-page PDF or a several-hundred-page book as often as you need.

Do I need to turn off grayscale for color pages?

Grayscale is on by default since most e-readers are grayscale or E Ink displays, but you can turn it off if you're reading on a color tablet like a Boox Tab or want to keep color diagrams intact.

Is the E-Reader Optimizer completely free?

Yes. PDFree is free with no subscription, no daily or hourly limits, and no signup. No watermark is added to the output and there's no file size restriction.
