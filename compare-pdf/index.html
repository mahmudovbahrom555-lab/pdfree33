When should you compare PDFs?

Check if a contract was altered after signing

A counterparty returned a signed PDF. Before relying on it, compare it against the version you sent. Any added clause, changed number, or removed paragraph shows up immediately in the diff view.

Review a lawyer's redline — find every change

Drop the draft you sent and the marked-up version that came back. PDFree highlights every revision in red so you can review the redline without toggling between tabs or printed copies.

Verify two PDFs are identical

Confirm a copy or certified duplicate matches the original before archiving. If every page shows 0% changed, the files are visually identical — useful for compliance, auditing, and document management workflows.

Compare document revisions (V1 vs V2)

Technical specifications, engineering drawings, policy documents — when a new version lands, compare it against the previous one to understand exactly what changed without reading both in full.

Proof-check a print-ready PDF

Compare the press-ready export against the design source to catch last-minute layout shifts, font substitutions, or colour changes before sending to print. The diff view catches what the eye misses.

Compare a scanned copy to the digital original

Check that a physical scan matches the source file — useful when verifying certified copies, checking that a faxed document arrived intact, or confirming no pages were added or removed during scanning.

Frequently asked questions

Will my files stay private?

Yes. PDFree runs entirely in your browser. Neither PDF is ever uploaded — not to PDFree's servers, not to any third party. Open DevTools → Network tab while comparing and you will not see your files being transmitted anywhere.

Can I compare password-protected PDFs?

Yes, if you know the password. PDFree prompts you to enter it when you open a protected file. Once unlocked, the comparison proceeds normally. PDFree cannot bypass or crack a password — it only opens files you are authorised to open.

Can I compare PDFs on iPhone or Mac without Adobe Acrobat?

Yes. Open PDFree in Safari (iPhone/iPad) or any browser on Mac — no app download or Adobe Acrobat required. Drop both PDFs, tap Compare PDFs, and scroll through the diff. Works entirely in the browser.

Can I compare scanned PDFs?

Yes. PDFree compares at the pixel level, so it works on scanned pages as well as digital PDFs. Any visible difference — a stamp, a handwritten note, a misaligned scan — is highlighted in the diff view.

Can I check if two PDFs are identical?

Yes. After comparing, pages with no differences show a 0% changed badge. If all pages show 0% and the page counts match, the files are visually identical — useful for compliance verification, certified copies, and archiving workflows.

What is a PDF redline?

A redline is a marked-up version of a document — typically a contract or legal agreement — that shows tracked changes compared to an earlier draft. The term comes from marking changes in red ink. PDFree's diff view serves the same purpose: drop the original and the revised version, and changed areas are highlighted in red.

Does it detect text changes or only visual differences?

PDFree performs a visual comparison — it highlights any pixel that differs between the two PDFs. This covers text edits, formatting changes, added images, stamps, and layout shifts. If you need text-only diffing, use OCR PDF first to extract the text, then a text-diff tool.

Can I compare PDFs with different page counts?

Yes. PDFree compares all pages that exist in both files. Pages that only appear in one PDF are shown with an "Only in A" or "Only in B" badge so you immediately see which document has extra content.

Can I export or download the comparison result?

No. PDFree displays the differences in your browser for review but does not generate a downloadable report or redline PDF. If you need to share the result, capture the relevant pages using your operating system's screenshot tool (Cmd+Shift+4 on Mac, Windows+Shift+S on Windows). You can then use Redact PDF to cover sensitive areas before sending.
