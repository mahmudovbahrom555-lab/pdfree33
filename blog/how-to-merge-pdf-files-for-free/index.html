Common reasons to merge PDF files

  • Contracts + addenda: Clients often sign main agreements and amendments as separate files. Merge them into one before archiving or uploading to a contract management system.
  • Academic submissions: Many university portals accept only one PDF file. Combine your cover page, paper body, appendices, and bibliography into a single submission.
  • Scanned multi-page documents: If you scanned a document one page at a time, merge all individual page PDFs into one complete document.
  • Client reports: Assemble an executive summary, data tables, charts, and supporting materials into one professional PDF to share with clients.
  • Invoice batches: Combine multiple invoices into a single PDF for batch processing or record-keeping.

Is it safe to merge confidential PDFs online?

Yes — when you use PDFree. Your files are merged entirely inside your browser using JavaScript and WebAssembly. No data is transmitted to any server. Contracts, financial documents, medical records, and legal files stay on your device throughout the process.

Frequently asked questions

How many PDF files can I merge at once?

There is no hard limit. PDFree processes everything in browser memory, so you can merge dozens of files. For very large batches (50+ files or 500MB+ total), close other browser tabs to free up RAM.

Will fonts and formatting be preserved after merging?

Yes. PDFree copies pages at the PDF object level — fonts, images, vector graphics, and layouts are preserved exactly as in the originals.

Can I merge PDF files on my phone?

Yes. PDFree works in mobile browsers — Chrome for Android and Safari for iOS. Use the file picker to add your PDFs and merge them directly on your phone.

What if the merged PDF is too large to email?

Use PDFree's Compress tool to reduce the merged file size. Typical reduction is 40–60% for image-heavy documents.
