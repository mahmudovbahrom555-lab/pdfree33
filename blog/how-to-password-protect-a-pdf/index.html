What does PDF password protection actually do?

When you add a password to a PDF, the document is encrypted using RC4-128 — the PDF format's Revision 3 standard security handler. The encryption scrambles the file contents so that without the correct password, the file appears as random bytes that cannot be read, copied, printed, or extracted. Note this is not AES-256 — for regulated or extremely sensitive documents, pair it with an additional safeguard rather than relying on it alone.

Important: a password-protected PDF is still a standard PDF file. Anyone with a modern PDF viewer (Adobe Acrobat, macOS Preview, Foxit, any browser) can open it — they will just be prompted for the password first.

When to use PDF password protection

  • Contracts and legal documents: Before emailing a signed contract, encrypt it and share the password by phone or a separate message.
  • Tax returns and financial statements: Bank statements, payslips, and tax documents contain sensitive data — encrypt before storing in cloud folders or sending to accountants.
  • Medical records: Patient records and health documents should be restricted to authorized readers.
  • Passport scans and ID copies: Identity documents are prime targets for theft — always encrypt before sending by email.

Is it safe to encrypt a PDF on a website?

With most online PDF tools, no — they upload your document to their servers to process it. Your password and your confidential PDF are transmitted to a third party.

PDFree is different. The entire encryption process runs in your browser using JavaScript and WebAssembly (pdf-lib). Your PDF and your password are never transmitted anywhere. PDFree literally cannot see either — the encryption keys are generated and applied entirely on your device. This is verifiable: PDFree is open source (GNU AGPLv3).
