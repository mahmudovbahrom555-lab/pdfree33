Why PDFree can offer OCR with no limit

Cloud OCR tools process your file on their own servers, which costs them compute time per document — that's why most ration free usage to a few tasks a day, or reserve it for paying customers. PDFree's OCR runs via Tesseract.js, a WebAssembly build of the open-source Tesseract engine, executing on your own device's CPU. There's no server-side cost per OCR job, so there's no reason to ration it.

The tradeoff: recognition speed depends on your device rather than a data-center server, so very large documents may take longer on an older laptop or phone than they would on a cloud service's hardware. For typical documents — a few dozen pages — the difference is a matter of seconds, not minutes.

What to actually look for in a free OCR tool

  • Real limits, not marketing limits. "Free" often means "free for one file" or "free for 2 files a day" — check the fine print before committing to a workflow.
  • Language support. Some free tools only recognize English well; if your documents are in another language, verify support before relying on the tool.
  • Where the file goes. If the document is sensitive — medical, legal, financial — a tool that requires upload adds a data-handling question that a browser-based tool avoids entirely.
  • Output quality. Some free tiers watermark the output or cap resolution; verify the downloaded file is unrestricted.
