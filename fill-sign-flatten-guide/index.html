Fill, Sign & Flatten a PDF — the Complete Workflow

Three steps, two tools, zero uploads. Here's exactly how the pieces fit together.

Why This Is a Two-Tool Workflow, Not One

Filling out a form and locking it afterward are genuinely different operations, and PDFree keeps them as two focused tools rather than bundling them into one:

  • Fill (/fill/) — enter text into form fields, check boxes, pick dropdown values, and place a signature.
  • Flatten (/flatten-pdf/) — take a filled document and permanently bake every value into the page, removing the ability to edit it further.

A form you've just filled in is still, technically, a form — the fields are populated but still editable by anyone who opens the file next. That's exactly what you want while you're double-checking your own entries. It's not what you want once the document is ready to send. Flattening is the step that turns "editable form with my answers in it" into "final document."

The Full Walkthrough

1
Fill in the form
Open pdfree.io/fill/ and upload your PDF. Detected fields — text boxes, checkboxes, dropdowns — become clickable. Fill in your information.
2
Add your signature
In the same tool, open the signature pad and choose Draw, Type, or Upload. Place it on the signature line.
3
Download the filled, signed PDF
This version has your answers and signature in it — but the underlying fields still exist and are technically still editable by whoever opens it next. Keep this copy in case you need to make a correction later.
4
Flatten it
Open pdfree.io/flatten-pdf/ and upload the file from step 3. Flattening bakes every field's value into the page content permanently and strips out the editable form structure.
5
Download the final PDF
This is the file you send. Your answers and signature are locked in — nobody, including you, can edit the fields on this copy anymore.

Keep the pre-flatten copy. Flattening isn't reversible — once a field is baked into the page, that particular file can't be un-flattened. Hold onto the version from step 3 until you're certain everything is correct.

When Flattening Actually Matters

📄
Contracts and agreements

Once both parties sign, neither side should be able to quietly change a term afterward.

🧾
Government or HR forms

Submitted intake forms and applications should reflect exactly what was submitted, permanently.

✅
Consent and waiver forms

A signed waiver that can still be edited defeats the purpose of having it signed at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why flatten a PDF after filling and signing it?

Without flattening, a filled-in form's fields are still editable — anyone who opens it can change what you typed or move your signature, even after saving. Flattening bakes every value into permanent page content and removes the interactive form structure.

Do I have to use two separate tools for this?

Yes — Fill and Flatten are two focused tools rather than one combined tool. Fill handles data entry and signature placement; Flatten handles locking the result. Running the filled file through Flatten as a second step takes under a minute.

Is flattening reversible if I made a mistake?

No — once flattened, the original interactive fields are gone from that file. Keep the filled-but-not-yet-flattened version from step 3 until you're sure everything is correct.

Does this workflow require uploading my document anywhere?

No. Both the Fill and Flatten tools run entirely in your browser — your document never leaves your device at any step.
